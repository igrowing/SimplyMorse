# SimplyMorse

Turn your phone into a Morse code station. Type a message and send it as sound or light — or point it at a beeping speaker or a blinking lamp and watch the words appear. The whole conversation, dots and dashes included, happens right in your pocket.

## What It Does

**Send as Sound** — Type any message and the app taps it out as clean Morse beeps. Pick the speed from 1 to 40 words per minute and tune the pitch anywhere from 400 to 1000 Hz until it sounds just right. Your settings are remembered for next time.

**Send as Light** — The same message, blinked out on the phone's flashlight (LED). Perfect for signalling across a dark field, a lake, or a room — no sound required. On the web version, an on-screen LED stands in for the torch.

**Send as Both** — Beep and blink together, so the person on the other end can hear it *and* see it. Great for teaching, demos, or just making sure the message gets through.

**Live Transmission View** — As your message goes out, the text lights up letter by letter in sync with the beeps and flashes. You always know exactly where the transmission is — and it doubles as a gentle way to learn the rhythm of the code.

**Message History** — Every message you send is saved to a quick-pick list. Sending "SOS" or "QRZ?" or your callsign again is one tap away.

**Hear It (decode audio)** — Point the microphone at a Morse signal — a practice oscillator, a shortwave radio, another phone — and SimplyMorse listens. It calibrates for a couple of seconds, locks onto the tone (and shows you the exact frequency it found), tracks the sender's speed automatically, and types out the decoded text as it goes. No need to know the pitch or the WPM in advance — it figures both out.

**Watch It (decode light)** — Aim the camera at a blinking light and the app reads the flashes as Morse. It finds the blinking spot in the frame, follows it even when your hand is shaking, and converts the on/off pattern into text. A live camera preview with a "scanning" indicator shows you what it's locked onto.

**Pause, Resume, Edit** — Decoding runs until you tap Pause, and picks right back up on Resume without losing what you've got. The decoded text sits in an editable box, so you can fix a stray character, then Copy it or Share it straight to any app.

**A Real Part of Your Survival Kit** — When the mobile network is down, the power is out, or you're out of shouting range, Morse still gets through. A flashlight blinking *SOS*, a beep pattern across a valley, a tapped message on a pipe — these carry in conditions where calls and data fail completely. SimplyMorse lets you both send that signal and read the one coming back, using only the phone already in your pocket and its battery. Whether you're seriously prepared for the worst or just someone who would rather not be caught helpless, this is an app worth installing long before the day you need it.

**Adaptive and Hands-Off** — Both decoders estimate the noise floor, confirm a signal before committing to it, and keep re-scanning in the background. If the sender changes tone, drifts, or you drift off target, the app quietly re-locks on its own.

**Full International Morse** — Letters, digits, and the full punctuation set (period, comma, question mark, slash, parentheses, colon, plus, equals, @ and more) in both directions.

**Theme Support** — Light, dark, or follow the system. The camera preview always shows the real scene; everything else adapts.

**Works Offline, Everywhere** — No account, no sign-up, no Internet. SimplyMorse never makes a single network request. Runs on Android, iOS, and in the browser (audio sending and decoding work on the web; light decoding needs a phone camera).

## Why You'll Want It

Morse code is the original text message — a way to get a few words across with nothing but a buzzer, a flashlight, or a tapped finger. It still works when the cell network is down, when you're too far to shout, or when you just want a signalling trick that feels a little bit magic.

SimplyMorse gives you both ends of that conversation. Send a message to a friend across a campsite with your flashlight. Leave the app listening while a practice tape plays and check how much you got right. Flash "HELLO" at your kid from across the yard and watch them decode it. Sit in on a net with a shortwave radio and let the app keep up with the fast operators while you learn.

For anyone studying for a ham radio license, the live transmission view turns every message you send into a lesson — you *see* the timing while you hear it. And for the merely curious, it's the fastest way to answer "wait, what does my name sound like in Morse?"

But the reason to install it *now*, while you don't need it, is simple: it belongs in your survival kit. When a storm takes out the towers, when you're lost past cell range, when you can see rescuers but can't reach them by voice, a phone that can blink and beep Morse — and decode the reply — becomes a real lifeline. You don't have to be preparing for the end of the world to want that. You just have to have been in trouble once.

It's free, it's fast, and it turns the phone you already carry into a two-way Morse station.

| C | `-.-.` | | L | `.-..` | | U | `..-` |
| D | `-..` | | M | `--` | | V | `...-` |

### Punctuation & Special Symbols

| Symbol | Code | | Symbol | Code |
|--------|------|-|--------|------|
| `.` (period) | `.-.-.-` | | `+` (plus) | `.-.-.` |
| `,` (comma) | `--..--` | | `-` (hyphen) | `-....-` |
| `?` (question) | `..--..` | | `_` (underscore) | `..--.-` |
| `'` (apostrophe) | `.----.` | | `"` (quote) | `.-..-.` |
| `!` (exclamation) | `-.-.--` | | `$` (dollar) | `...-..-` |
| `/` (slash) | `-..-.` | | `@` (at sign) | `.--.-.` |
| `(` (open paren) | `-.--.` | | `&` (ampersand) | `.-...` |

| `;` (semicolon) | `-.-.-.` | | `=` (equals) | `-...-` |

> **Space** between words is represented as a pause — no code symbol, just a gap longer than a normal letter break.
